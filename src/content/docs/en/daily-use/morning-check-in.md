The morning check-in is the smallest piece of work Corpus asks of you — under ten seconds — and the highest-leverage. It's the bridge between what your body did overnight (which the watch already captured) and what *you* did yesterday (which the watch can't see).

## What you'll see

Each morning, three questions about yesterday. They're tap-to-answer — yes/no, low/moderate/high, or a small pick list. Examples:

- *Did you have caffeine after 3pm yesterday?*
- *Any alcohol?*
- *Stress at work — low, moderate, or high?*
- *Was your last meal heavy or late?*

Below the questions, Corpus shows your **daily coaching comment** — two to four sentences interpreting your overnight data, your recent context, and what to focus on today. It's specific. It cites things ("third night in a row of sub-target sleep", "training load ratio above 1.3").

## Why three questions, and why those three

The questions adapt over time. In your first weeks, Corpus asks about the things known from research to matter most: caffeine timing, alcohol, screen time, stress. As your **personalised insights** start surfacing — *your* triggers, not generic ones — the questions zero in on whatever has the largest effect on your numbers. If late caffeine knocks 28% off your deep sleep, you'll see "late caffeine?" pretty often.

## What it actually drives

- **Today's coaching comment** uses your answers as part of the daily context.
- The **behaviour engine** stores yes/high/late answers as logged tags, and pairs them with what your body did over the next 24–48 hours.
- After two to three weeks of consistent answers, the **insights** start appearing — statistically meaningful patterns from *your* data.

## What if you skip a day

Nothing breaks. Days without a check-in just don't contribute new signal to the insight engine — which means longer to first insights, and less granular ones. The system tolerates gaps; it just learns slower.

## A note on honesty

The check-in is private and on-device. There's no judgement, no streak shaming. The only consumer of the answers is the engine that's trying to figure out what works for you — so the more honest the answer, the more useful the result.
