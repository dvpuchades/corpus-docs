A weekly plan that's good in isolation isn't enough — the weeks have to add up. The roadmap is how Corpus keeps that progression coherent over months.

## The plan summary

In your profile, Corpus maintains a short paragraph it calls the **plan summary** — a few sentences describing where you are in the journey towards your goal, what this phase is trying to achieve, and what comes next. Example:

> *Week 6 of 16 toward the half marathon on 12 October. Building aerobic base; long runs at conversational pace, two interval sessions per week. Deload scheduled for week 8. Recomposition is a secondary goal — keeping protein high and lifting twice a week to preserve lean mass through the cardio block.*

This summary is what keeps weeks consistent. When the coach generates next week's plan, it reads this summary first. When you ask the chat coach a question, it has this context. When you change your goal, the summary rewrites itself.

You can edit it. If the model has miscounted what you're trying to do, fix the summary directly and future plans align.

## Periodisation, automatically

For time-bound goals, Corpus periodises:

- **Build phases** — progressive overload of the relevant load (mileage for runners, volume for lifters, both for hybrid goals).
- **Deload weeks** — typically every 3–4 weeks, where load drops while quality stays.
- **Taper** — the last 1–3 weeks before a deadline, intensity preserved while volume cuts.
- **Recovery blocks** — after a deadline event, the plan defaults to a structured re-entry rather than dumping you straight back into building.

For open-ended goals, Corpus runs a steady-state structure: predictable week-to-week load, tuned to whatever your readiness can sustain over time.

## Multiple goals

You can have more than one. *"Half marathon in October"* + *"Recomposition"* will produce a plan that does both, with a clear bias when they conflict — typically toward the closer deadline. The plan summary tells you which is being prioritised in the current phase.

## When the roadmap changes

Three things make the roadmap shift:

- **A new goal** — replaces or joins the existing structure.
- **A meaningful change in your data** — extended underrecovery, an injury reading from training-load ratio, persistently low sleep — all of which the coach treats as a signal to back off.
- **You ask it to.** Open chat and tell it. The coach has the same write access to your goal and your plan that it has to your weekly schedule.

## Reading the roadmap

In the goal detail view, you'll see:

- The **deadline** (or "open-ended").
- The **plan summary** (editable).
- A **timeline** of completed and upcoming phases.
- The **headline metric** the goal is being judged against — race time, body composition, sleep consistency, or something custom.

That's it. The roadmap deliberately doesn't drown you in a 16-week spreadsheet — the granular planning happens at the week level, week by week, with full context.
