Of the six daily metrics, **readiness, strain and recovery** are the trio that drive most of how the plan adapts. They're tightly coupled, and they're the ones the coaching comment leans on most.

## Readiness

A daily snapshot of how prepared your body is for physical and mental demands.

- **Levels:** Optimal · Good · Moderate · Low · Very Low.
- **What it reflects:** resting heart rate, heart-rate variability, recent sleep quality, recovery state, and current training load — combined.
- **What to do:** Optimal/Good — the planned session is fair game. Moderate — listen to your body; the coach typically proposes a slightly lighter session. Low/Very Low — recovery comes first; the plan auto-adjusts and the coach will say so explicitly.

Readiness is the most "is today the day?" of the metrics. It's not just fitness — it's everything the autonomic nervous system can see. A poor night's sleep, a stressful work day, an oncoming illness, undertraining showing as a tanking baseline — all show up here.

## Strain

How much cumulative physical load your body has absorbed today.

- **Levels:** Minimal · Light · Moderate · High · Maximal.
- **What it reflects:** workout intensity, duration, and frequency combined with all-day movement and physiological cost (heart-rate-based).
- **What to do:** check it against readiness. High strain with Low readiness is a signal to ease off — you're climbing a hole. Minimal strain with Good readiness is the opposite — your body is asking for work.

In the background, strain feeds the **training load ratio**: acute (last 7 days) vs. chronic (last 28). Above ~1.3 means you're ramping fast — injury risk goes up. Below ~0.8 means you're not training enough to make progress toward the goal. The plan and the coach both nudge you back into the productive band.

## Recovery

How well your body is bouncing back from strain and stress.

- **Levels:** Fully Recovered · Recovering · Underrecovered · Depleted.
- **What it reflects:** the *interplay* of sleep quality, strain, stress, and HRV trends over the recent days. It's an integrative read, not a single number.
- **What to do:** Depleted — the plan reflects this; expect a deload or an active recovery day. Underrecovered — a light session might be helpful or harmful depending on the cause; the coach will weigh in. Fully Recovered — green light. Recovering — typical day; do the planned session.

## How they work together

Imagine a busy work week with poor sleep:

- Strain stays moderate because you're still training.
- Stress climbs as HRV drops.
- Recovery slips from *Recovering* to *Underrecovered*.
- Readiness drops to Moderate, then Low.

By the time readiness is Low, the plan has already started backing off — the coach saw recovery slipping two days earlier. That's the loop working as intended.

## Mental load is part of this too

Stress is physiological, but it picks up mental and emotional load with surprising fidelity — HRV, RHR drift, sleep architecture. If your recovery is Depleted but you feel fine and your training has been light, look at what's happening *off* the watch: work, sleep timing, life. Corpus can't see those directly, but the data they leave on the autonomic nervous system is exactly what these three metrics are for.
