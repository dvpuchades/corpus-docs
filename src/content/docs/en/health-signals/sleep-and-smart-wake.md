Sleep is the biggest lever on every other metric in Corpus. It's also the one most often misread by simple "hours slept" tracking — duration alone misses *when*, *how broken*, and *how regular* your sleep was.

## What Corpus reads

Sleep quality is assessed across several dimensions, not just total time:

- **Total duration** vs. your personalised target (which adapts to your patterns).
- **Time in deep and REM** stages.
- **Interruptions and awakenings.**
- **Regularity** — bedtime and wake-time consistency across the week.
- **Sleep debt** — cumulative delta against your target, so you can see whether you're catching up or falling behind.
- **Naps** — daytime sleep is detected automatically and counted separately from your nighttime block.

The result is a single level — Excellent, Good, Fair or Poor — plus a sleep detail view with a 30-day schedule chart so you can see at a glance whether your bedtimes and wake times are drifting.

## What "Fair" or "Poor" means

The level isn't a guilt trip — it's a flag for the engine. A Fair sleep night feeds into lower readiness, slower recovery, and (over a few days) a lighter plan. The coach pulls related insights when relevant: *"Late caffeine logged yesterday — that's been associated with 28% less deep sleep for you."* If you have a hunch about what disrupted last night, the morning check-in is exactly the place to capture it.

## Smart wake

Smart wake is a notification that fires inside a window you set, picking a moment when you're in lighter sleep so waking up feels less brutal. It's not a separate alarm replacing your phone's — it works alongside it as a cue to start the day in a better part of the cycle.

How it works:

- You set a **window** (typically 15–30 minutes before your latest acceptable wake time).
- Corpus monitors sleep stage in real time.
- When the watch detects a transition out of deep sleep, the notification fires.
- If no good moment appears, it fires at the latest end of the window — you don't oversleep.

For the smart-wake notification to be useful, sleep with your watch on. Without overnight watch data, Corpus can still detect sleep windows from iPhone signals, but stage detection — which is what smart wake depends on — needs the watch.

## Practical guidance

- **The single highest-impact behaviour** for most people is bedtime *consistency*. A regular sleep schedule does more for readiness and recovery over a month than any one good night.
- **Naps count.** A 20–40 minute nap can recover surprisingly well from a short night; longer naps tend to push tonight's bedtime later and compound the problem.
- **Sleep debt is forgiving but slow.** A single late night doesn't show up much in next-day metrics; three in a row very much does.
- **Cycle phase matters** if you menstruate — Corpus factors it into recovery and readiness automatically when cycle data is available, so don't be surprised if your sleep needs and recovery rate look different week to week.
