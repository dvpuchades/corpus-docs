Corpus doesn't try to be a generic fitness app. It plans against a specific goal, which is why the first thing it asks for is one.

## What a good goal looks like

The two pieces that matter are **what** and **when**.

- "Half marathon on 12 October." → Corpus periodises a 16-week build, with deload weeks and a taper.
- "Recomposition over the next 12 weeks." → Lifting volume is prioritised; cardio is dosed so it doesn't blunt recovery; body-composition trends become the front-and-centre signal.
- "Sleep more consistently." → Strain is dialled to whatever your readiness can support; bedtime/wake-time consistency becomes a tracked outcome.
- "Less work stress, better evenings." → The plan shifts towards aerobic and mobility work; high-intensity is gated on stress signals.

You don't need a deadline. Open-ended goals (e.g. *"feel better day to day"*) work; they just shift the plan from a build/peak shape to a steady-state shape.

## What Corpus actually does with the goal

- The **weekly plan** is shaped to it — session types, intensities, and total load all depend on it.
- The **coach** uses it as the lens for everything: when you ask "should I run today?", the answer balances readiness *and* what the goal needs from you this week.
- The **plan summary** in your profile is a short paragraph the model maintains to keep weeks consistent. It updates as you progress.
- The **metrics** are interpreted in context: a "Moderate" strain reading means something different in a marathon-build week than it does in a deload week.

## Changing it

Goals are live. Change them in *Profile → Goals* and the next plan generation reshapes the week. You can have more than one — say, a marathon plus a recomposition target — and Corpus will negotiate between them when they pull in different directions (with bias, sensibly, towards whichever has the closer deadline).

## What if you don't know yet

Pick the closest thing to "what would make this year feel like a win", and edit it later. The cost of a wrong goal is one week of slightly off-target plans; the cost of no goal is that Corpus has nothing to plan towards.
